🎶 The Music
If you love pop, hip-hop, and K-pop, this place can be for you.
* Before midnight, the playlist is more relaxed — some familiar Western pop, the kind you can warm up to.
* But after midnight? That’s when it gets wild. Think BLACKPINK, BTS, Nicki, Cardi, you name it. The kind of music that makes you forget what time it is.
Everyone is dancing, and not in the awkward “sway and sip” kind of way — people here actually move. Sometimes there are spontaneous dance-offs. You’ll get pulled into a group dance without even realizing it.

💃 The Crowd
Most of the crowd is uni students and young professionals. You’ll meet locals, international students, and expats — and everyone’s there to have a good time. I’ve never felt judged or out of place here. It’s a friendly space, and people genuinely seem to be enjoying themselves.
It’s also one of the few clubs where people aren’t constantly trying to flirt or push up on you. You can just enjoy the night without any weird pressure.
💸 Cover & Tips
* Entry varies, but usually around ¥100 for guys (includes 1 drink)
* ¥159 for girls (includes 2 drinks)
* If you’ve got the Full Pass, no entry fee needed
If you’re coming on the weekend, get there on the earlier side — it does get packed. But it’s worth it.
✅ Final Thoughts
If your idea of a good night is dancing until your legs give out, this is the place. The music is solid, the energy is high, and there’s zero pretense.
Not the best spot for hookups or bottle service — but if you’re going out for the music and to let loose, Culture is the move.
Bring water, wear something you can move in, and prepare to lose track of time.

Hush:
If you’re into hip-hop, this place is basically your playground. it’s exactly what you’d expect from a hip-hop club — loud music, packed dance floor, and lots of energy. They don’t mess around with genres — it’s straight beats all night. The moment you walk in, you’re already moving because (1) the music hits hard, and (2) there’s zero room to just stand around. Seriously, the dance floor is so small you’ll accidentally be grinding on someone before you even realize what’s happening.
The crowd is mostly college students, all there to have a good time. If someone says they’re “just here to dance,” they’re probably not being totally honest. Everyone’s trying to meet people or at least have some fun.
One funny thing: the longest line — the bathroom line — is where a lot of the socializing actually happens. I saw a few people swap numbers while waiting, so keep that in mind.
Entry & Drinks
* Weekdays: ¥88
* Weekends: ¥158 The good part? Entry includes 8 basic cocktails. They’re nothing fancy, but after a few drinks, that doesn’t really matter.
If you have the Full Pass, you don’t need to pay for entry — you just walk right in.
Final Thoughts
If you want lots of space and a chill vibe, this isn’t the spot. The dance floor is small and packed tight, so expect to be close with strangers.
But if you want a lively night, real hip-hop music, and don’t mind the crowd, Hush is worth checking out. Just wear comfortable shoes and prepare for a late night.

Lafin:
So I checked out this club before that’s more about business and socializing than dancing. It’s mostly tables and seats, not much of a dance floor. There are quite a few college students too, but don’t expect any professional LinkedIn-style networking.
If you want to dance, the dance floor is really small—barely enough space to move around.
Heads up: it can get a bit handsy at times, so keep an eye on your personal space and look out for your friends.
Cover is about ¥150 on weekdays and ¥188 on weekends, both come with two drinks included. If you’ve got the Full Pass, you can skip the cover.
The music is mostly trap, R&B, and popular hip-hop and Western hits. The playlist is solid if you like that kind of music.
All in all, it’s a good spot if you want a mix of business vibes and music, but don’t expect a full-on dance party.

Radi:
So I hit up this club in Shanghai that’s basically an EDM and pop party playground. Seriously, foreign hotties everywhere — you might wanna brush up on your “hello” in a few languages because you’re gonna hear it a lot.
The dance floor? Sticky as hell — like, the kind of sticky that tells you people have been going hard all night.
The crowd is mostly students who are here to have a good time — probably 80% — plus some models, college kids flexing their parents’ money, and a handful of exchange students who are “researching Chinese culture” (aka just drinking and dancing).
Cover’s usually around ¥120 on weekdays, includes one drink, and goes up to about ¥180 on weekends with a drink as well. If you’ve got the Full Pass, entry’s free, which is a nice bonus.
What really sets this place apart is the music. They bring in top DJs every month, and the energy is unreal. The resident DJs spin popular Western tracks flipped into house remixes that keep the floor alive from night till 4 or 5 a.m. Nonstop dancing, no breaks — if you’re looking to burn some serious energy, this is the spot.

Other Popular Clubs in Shanghai, not inside INS Park:
Orii:
Orii opened in late 2022, and it's quickly become one of those places in Shanghai — where students, K-pop fans, and curious expats all end up on a Friday night. I’ve been a couple of times now, and here’s how I’d sum it up: fun and loud
Atmosphere & Crowd
The second you walk in, the vibe is great. The lighting is bold, mostly pink tones with a kind of digital-pixel theme that actually photographs really well. You’ll want to grab a picture before the dance floor fills up — and it will fill up fast.
Most of the crowd is on the younger side — lots of university students and people who clearly know the BTS choreography better than I do. You’ll hear a mix of Mandarin, English, and the occasional overly-loud “ni hao” from someone trying a little too hard. But overall, it’s friendly, open, and people are there to have fun.
Music
The music changes depending on the time. Before midnight, it’s mostly Western pop — think Top 40 hits, familiar tunes, a little electronic. It’s good for warming up, but the real party starts after midnight.
That’s when the K-pop kicks in. BLACKPINK, BTS, EXO, XG — you name it. The whole place turns into a live music video. Even if you’re not a huge K-pop fan, it’s hard not to get swept up in the energy. The bass hits, the fog machines go off, and the dance floor literally bounces (they’ve got a springy floor — pretty unique).
Drinks & Entry
* Cover: ¥100 on weekdays, ¥150 on weekends (includes a drink)
* Ladies: Free drinks before midnight
* Drinks are pretty standard. Don’t expect craft cocktails, but they do the job.
Tips if You Go
* Arrive early — 11:30pm or earlier if you don’t want to wait in line.
* Don’t plan on quiet conversations — it gets loud fast.
* Charge your phone — the lighting makes for great photos, especially near the bar or by the stage.
* Be open to dancing — even if you don’t usually dance, you probably will here.
Final Thoughts
Orii isn’t the most refined nightclub, but that’s not really the point. It’s fun, it’s packed, and it’s one of the better places in town if you want to dance, hear K-pop played loud, and stay out way later than planned.
Not a weekly hangout, but definitely a fun one to experience at least once — especially if you're into music, energy, and letting loose.
